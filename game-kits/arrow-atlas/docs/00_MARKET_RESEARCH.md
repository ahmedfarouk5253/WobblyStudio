# 00: Market Research: the arrow-escape genre (October 2026)

Research date: 3 Oct 2026. Sources are listed at the end. Labels used below:
- **[data]**: a store or market-intelligence figure.
- **[reviews]**: patterns from about 45 recent App Store reviews across 8 arrow games.
- **[analysis]**: our own reading of the evidence.

---

## 1. The genre in one paragraph

Each piece on the board is a line of cells, straight or bent, with an arrowhead at one end. When you tap an arrow, the game checks one thing: is the **straight line from the head to the edge of the board** empty? If yes, the head flies out and the body follows its own path, snake-style. If no, the arrow bumps, slides back, and you lose a heart. Clear every arrow to win. Boards start fully packed, so at first only arrows on the edge can leave. The puzzle is reading which arrows are free. There are no timers. Levels take 15–60 seconds early on, and several minutes on huge late boards.

## 2. Size of the opportunity [data]

- **Worldwide download charts.** Arrow puzzles held 2–3 of the world's **top-5 most-downloaded mobile games** in April, May and June 2026.
- **June 2026:**
  - #1 Arrows – Puzzle Escape (Lessmore), 25.3M installs
  - #2 Amaze GO! (Oakever), 23.2M
  - #5 Arrow Puzzle – Tap Away (Easybrain), 16.4M
- **Arrows (Lessmore):**
  - Launched in Aug 2025 by a small German studio.
  - Now 100M+ on Google Play.
  - Rated 4.8★ from about 470K ratings on iOS.
- **Puzzle as a category** grew about 20% to $8.1B in H1 2026. Hybrid-casual grew about 23%. Sort, screw and block puzzles together made about $600M IAP in H1 2026.
- **Download leaders earn mostly from ads.** Block Blast! has 830M+ installs but almost zero IAP revenue. The arrow leaders follow the same pattern: ad-first, with Remove Ads as the main purchase.
- **India, Brazil and Indonesia** drive downloads but pay little per ad. The US is about 7% of downloads but about 29% of spending.

**[analysis] Why Lessmore won:**
1. They were first to market with a clean version of the format.
2. Early levels are very fast, so a new player gets 19 wins in 15 minutes.
3. The format is easy to show in a 6-second video ad ("only 1% can solve this").
4. They paid for installs heavily in April–May 2026.
5. They built a calm, premium-looking brand.

What separates the leaders from the near-identical clones: *"All three run close variants of the same core loop. What separated the top-ranked title was pacing and monetization timing."* (Emhance playtest)

## 4. Who's losing, and why [data + analysis]

- **A wave of copycats.** In 2026, "nearly ten copycat games" followed Lessmore within months, plus dozens of tiny ones. Many sit at 10K–100K installs; one "Arrows Go!" has **840 total downloads**.
- **Why the clones flop:**
  1. **Same game, worse.** Same mechanic, same white arrows, nothing new. Google Play's *Repetitive Content* policy explicitly rejects apps that "provide the same experience as other apps already on Google Play".
  2. **Keyword-stuffed listings with generic icons** (e.g. "Arrow Escape Puzzle Maze Tap Away Game"). These don't convert, and look spammy.
  3. **Aggressive monetization from day 1:** energy gates at level 10, fail-offer packs, $49.99 coin packs, banners over the board. Ratings drop, Google ranks the game lower, and organic installs die.
  4. **No paid acquisition and no video marketing**, in a genre whose hits are carried by short video ads.
  5. **Bugs:** taps landing on the wrong arrow, crashes on big levels, progress lost.
  6. **"Real money" gimmick clones** ("Arrow Escape – Earn Cash"). These attract low-quality traffic and get policy risk.
- **Bad updates kill good games.** Removing the grid, cutting ad rewards and adding promo screens are the moments players say they uninstalled.

## 5. What players LOVE [reviews], most mentioned first

1. **The right challenge.** It makes you think, you never feel stuck, and it's "not too hard".
2. **Relaxing.** People use it for anxiety, before bed, in hospital. No timer, play at your own pace.
3. **Fair ads, or Remove Ads that's worth paying for.** Players happily paid $8–10 when it really removed ads.
4. **Addictive in a good way.** "An hour or two passed."
5. **Grid dots and guide lines.** The most emotionally attached feature. Removing it immediately costs stars.
6. **Offline play, saved progress.**
7. **Colored arrows on a dark background.**
8. **Optional depth:** daily and monthly challenges, leagues, timed mode, as long as they stay *optional*.
9. **Juice:** haptics, the whoosh, the "dopamine bump".

## 6. What players HATE [reviews], most mentioned first

1. **Ads:** after every level, banners covering the board, 40–50 ads an hour in the worst cases, malicious ads.
2. **Mis-taps and big boards.** Zoom in and you can't see the puzzle; zoom out and you can't tap the right arrow. "99% of my lost health is from the wrong arrow getting hit."
3. **Bad updates:** rewards cut, free aids moved behind a paywall, themes removed.
4. **Too easy, repetitive, no difficulty choice.** "Hard just means more arrows crammed in." "Let me play Nightmare all the time."
5. **Punishing hearts, no undo.** "You really only have two times to mess up." "Can we get a no-health mode?"
6. **Visual comfort:** no dark mode, white-only arrows, no color choice.
7. **Bugs:** crashes, black screens on big levels, no mid-level save.
8. **Paid Remove Ads but still see ads**, and the Remove Ads button stays visible after purchase.
9. **Rewarded ad didn't pay out**, or asked for a second ad.
10. **Remove Ads price.** $7.99–$16.99 felt "greedy" to some; $3.99–$4.99 was suggested.
11. **Forced competitions** you can't opt out of.

Interesting finding: **players complain far more about "too easy" than "too hard".** Very few reviews mention impossible levels.

## 7. Monetization benchmarks seen in the genre [data]

- **Remove Ads:**
  - $7.99 at most clones.
  - $9.99–$16.99 at Lessmore and Easybrain.
  - Lessmore also sells *time-limited* ad removal: 1 month $9.99, 3 months $22.99, 6 months $39.99.
- **Other purchases:** coin ladders $0.99–$49.99, revive and "fail offer" packs $3.99–$5.99, starter packs $1.99–$2.99, hint and life refills $1.99, streak freezes $1.49.
- **Rewarded video** is offered for hints, extra hearts and doubled coins. The best timing (Lessmore) is to offer the hint **when the player stalls**, not on a timer.
- **Hybrid-casual norms:** a soft currency, a meta layer switched on by day 3–5, and the first purchase offer shown after a *success* rather than a failure (Pixel Flow pattern).

## 8. Retention reference points [data]
- Puzzle D30 retention is about 18% (mobile average about 13%). Puzzle D90 is about 10% (Color Block Jam).
- Women play puzzle games slightly longer than men. Players over 50 play more minutes a day than players in their 30s and 40s. This is a big, high-value audience that cares about readability.

## 9. Our conclusions → design decisions

| Evidence | Decision (see doc) |
|---|---|
| Mis-taps on big boards are the #1 gameplay pain | **Smart Touch**: snap-to-arrow hit testing, an ambiguity guard, a minimum on-screen cell size, minimap, 2-finger pan (02, 07) |
| Punishing hearts, no undo | 3 hearts + **Mistake Undo** (free once per level, then by rewarded ad) + **Zen mode** with no hearts (02) |
| Ads after every level | **Fair Ads Pledge**: first interstitial after level 15, then at most 1 every 3 levels and 3 minutes, never after a fail, no banners (06) |
| Remove Ads doesn't remove ads | Remove Ads $3.99 removes ALL interstitials, also turns Mistake Undo into a free refill 3×/day, and its button disappears once owned (06) |
| "Too easy / repetitive / more arrows" | **A new mechanic every 25 levels** (8 mechanics) + a difficulty curve with peaks + a choice of difficulty in the Daily puzzle and in Endless (03, 04) |
| Grid and colors are loved | Dot grid, aim line, dark mode and colored arrows are **free forever** and stated in the store listing (01, 08) |
| Calm / ASMR positioning works | Whoosh sounds, soft haptics, no timers, Zen mode (08) |
| Amaze GO!'s zoom-reveal was the best emotional beat | **Atlas meta:** clearing levels reveals a hand-painted travel postcard; landmark levels are shaped like the place (05) |
| Short video ads carry the genre | A share-a-replay GIF in v1.1 + an ad-creative plan (15, 17) |
| Clones get rejected or ignored | Our own name, mascot, art, mechanics and generator. No copied levels, art or listing text (01, 15) |
